**Introduction**

As artificial intelligence and Large Language Models reshape how we access digital information, digital humanists face a crucial challenge: how can we share rare historical archives openly with scholars while protecting our cultural heritage from unauthorized, commercial AI web scraping? During my two-week ATRIUM Transnational Access (TNA) fellowship at the Athens University of Economics and Business (AUEB), I set out to address this exact question. Hosted by the UNESCO Chair on Digital Methods for the Humanities and Social Sciences, my project focused on preserving and safely publishing the digitized archive of ***Teatarski glasnik*** (*Theatrical Herald*) - one of North Macedonia’s premier 20th-century theatrical periodicals.

**Preserving Cultural Heritage for Open Science**

The *Teatarski glasnik* collection captures decades of rich theatrical and cultural history, spanning over 800,000 lines and nearly 5 million words. Our primary goal was to convert raw historical scans into an open, machine-readable text corpus accessible to European researchers. Rather than relying on aggressive automated correction tools that risk erasing historical spellings, dialectal features, and regional proper names, we applied a gentle, dictionary-free auditing approach. This preserved the authentic linguistic identity of the archive while standardizing the text for modern computational research.

**Smart Protection for Open Heritage**

While Open Science encourages sharing research freely, publishing large text collections online can make them vulnerable to commercial web crawlers harvesting data without permission. To share this archive safely, we developed a digital watermarking system that acts as a subtle fingerprint for the corpus. This system allows us to verify data origin and trace whether the text has been harvested for commercial AI training — all while keeping the text completely readable and freely available to human scholars. The dataset is now safely published on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/22917991) under a Creative Commons CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license.
