**General Information**

Spoken language is the most important carrier of information in many research areas, in field work and in everyday life. The [TranscriptionPortal](https://clarin.phonetik.uni-muenchen.de/TranscriptionPortal/) makes this information accessible via a web service. It's as easy as 1-2-3: 1) upload your audio, 2) select the input and output languages, 3) start processing – and watch how your data makes its way through the matrix.

The [TranscriptionPortal](https://clarin.phonetik.uni-muenchen.de/TranscriptionPortal/) features a modern graphical user interface, and it currently supports five languages: English, Dutch, Italian, German and Greek. The typical workflow consists of automatic speech recognition, followed by optional manual validation, translation and summarization. For maximum usability for non-technical users, the individual services are preconfigured, so that only a very small set of options needs to be set. The outcome is a downloadable set of text files with the transcribed and translated text, in full length or as a summary.

The use of the TranscriptionPortal is free for academic institutions. To ensure high standards of privacy, data is stored only during processing, all communication is encrypted, and only trusted academic service providers are accessed.

The TranscriptionPortal is being operated by the Bavarian Archive of Speech Signals in close collaboration with CLST in Nijmegen, and is supported by CLARIN. In November 2025, it was evaluated successfully in a usability test.

**Christoph Draxler** is the head of the Bavarian Archive for Speech Signals at LMU Munich. His main research interests are speech databases, transcription, and the development of software for speech technology.

**Henk van den Heuvel** is a senior researcher and Head of the Humanities Lab at Radboud University.
